## Three things in your dictated version I changed, and why

Read this part before you record anything.

**1. "It loses on average 4 to 5 percent per year" and "almost 20 percent since 2019" do not agree with each other, and neither one is the real figure.**

The real figure is better than both. US CPI was 257.346 in October 2019 and 333.918 in July 2026. Prices rose 29.8 percent. That means a dollar held through it buys **22.9 percent less**, which annualises to **3.8 percent a year** over those 6.75 years.

So: **$100,000 held in cash since October 2019 buys about $77,000 worth of what it bought then.** Say seventy seven, not eighty. Say close to four percent a year, not four to five.

One caution you should hold in your head and not say on camera: this particular six year window ran hotter than normal. Long-run US inflation is nearer 3 percent. If someone senior asks you in a comment whether cash always loses 4 percent a year, the answer is no, this window did.

Sources, both ends published, both linked on the page now: [October 2019 release](https://www.bls.gov/news.release/archives/cpi_11132019.htm), [July 2026 release](https://www.bls.gov/news.release/cpi.nr0.htm).

**2. "Safe stocks and equities."** Stocks and equities are the same word. And in front of a finance audience on LinkedIn, calling equities safe is the kind of line that gets you one polite comment and quietly costs you credibility with everyone who did not comment. Say **broad funds that hold thousands of companies at once**. That is the actual claim, it is true, and it is more impressive.

**3. "It forecasts you a strategy."** It does not forecast anything, and you do not want to say on the internet that you built something that forecasts. It applies published assumptions from J.P. Morgan and a 126 year history series and shows you both. Say **it builds you a plan**. Same idea, and defensible.

Same for "geographical preferences": it does not ask where you would like to be. It asks which currency, which kind of institution, whether you could withdraw it tomorrow, and whether it can leave the country. That is more specific and more interesting than a preference.

## The script

**Bold lines are anchors. Say those close to word for word.** Everything else is a beat, not a line. Say it how you would say it to a friend.

Roughly 245 words. That is a touch long for 90 seconds. If you run over, cut the private bank beat first, it is the only one that is about you rather than about the viewer.

### Beat 1, 0:00 to 0:10. Face, full frame.

> **If you are young, you have a bit of money saved, and you have no idea what to do with it, I built a tool for you.**
>
> It is called the Mini Wealth Manager. It is free, and it is on my website.

Say it to one person, not to an audience. Slightly warmer than you think you need to be.

### Beat 2, 0:10 to 0:22. Face, full frame.

> I spent this summer inside a private bank in Beirut, working on portfolios for clients whose accounts start in the millions.
>
> **Private banking works. It just starts at about half a million dollars, and that is arithmetic, not a complaint.** So I built the small version.

The whole point of "that is arithmetic, not a complaint" is that it takes the resentment out of the line. Say it lightly, almost as an aside. If you say it bitterly it becomes the thing people remember.

### Beat 3, 0:22 to 0:34. Screen comes up, face shrinks to the corner.

> It starts with everything you have, not just a savings account. Cash at home, money in a bank, gold, things you cannot sell tomorrow.
>
> **And it asks which currency, which kind of bank, and whether you could actually take it out tomorrow morning.** In Lebanon those are four different questions.

### Beat 4, 0:34 to 0:46. On "What standing still costs".

> Because money sitting still does not stay the same size. It stays the same number.
>
> **A hundred thousand dollars in cash since October 2019 buys about seventy seven thousand dollars worth of what it bought then.** That is close to four percent a year, gone, for doing nothing.

Both index values are on screen behind you when you say this. Let the shot hold after the line instead of talking over it.

### Beat 5, 0:46 to 1:00. On "Before the market".

> Now here is the part I care about most. Before it shows you a single fund, it does one piece of division.
>
> **One extra dollar a month of income is worth about two hundred dollars invested.**
>
> So if you do not have a steady income yet, the best thing you can buy is not a fund. It is the laptop, or the course, or the certificate that gets you that income. Build the income first, then the investing actually compounds, because it repeats every month.

> **Read the number off your own screen.** That two hundred moves with whichever portfolio the example lands on. If your screen says 171, say a hundred and seventy. Never round a number up on camera, and never say a figure you have not just read.

### Beat 6, 1:00 to 1:14. Portfolio, then bad years.

> Then you get the portfolio itself. **Broad funds that hold thousands of companies at once**, what each one costs you every year, and where every single number came from.
>
> And then it runs the whole thing through the bad years. 2008. Covid. The 1970s. And one that has not happened yet.

Move quickly here. This beat is proof that the thing is real, not an explanation.

### Beat 7, 1:14 to 1:28. Back to face, full frame.

> It is free, the method is open, and every figure in it has a source you can click.
>
> **Do not leave your money sitting there. Sitting still is a decision too.**

Stop there. No call to action out loud. The caption does that, and asking for the click is what makes a good video feel like an ad.
