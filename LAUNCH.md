## Primary post

I spent this summer inside a private bank.

Not the retail side. The private banking floor, where relationships start in the millions. Across the industry those minimums run from around half a million dollars at some houses to ten million at the largest. That is not a complaint. It is arithmetic, and it is why nobody below that number gets built anything.

I kept thinking about who that leaves out.

You are twenty something. You have money sitting around from an internship, a gift, a part time job. You do not know what to do with it and nobody has ever properly taught you. That is most people I know, and it was me before this year.

So I spent a weekend building the tool I wanted.

The idea behind it is one sentence: your cash is not the risk free asset.

Every mainstream portfolio tool assumes it is. Cash is the safe corner of the balance sheet, the thing everything else gets measured against. If you are in Lebanon, that assumption is doing an enormous amount of unexamined work. Same in Egypt, Nigeria, Argentina, Turkey. Deposits get trapped, currencies reprice, and the balance you can see is not the money you can withdraw.

So the Mini Wealth Manager asks a different question. Not how much you have. How much of it is actually yours to move.

Two people with the same seven thousand dollars get completely different answers. The one holding it in a bank that will not release it has a different first move from the one holding it somewhere it can travel. I have not found a mainstream tool that models that at all.

The part I care about most is the part that argues against investing.

At a 7 percent expected return, one dollar a month of extra income is worth about 171 dollars of invested capital. If you have four figures saved and no recurring income yet, an extra 500 a month is worth more than ten times everything you could put in the market. So before it shows you a single fund, the tool does that division with your own numbers and tells you to build the income first. A laptop that works, a certification a specific door requires, the first month of something you sell.

You have to build the income before compounding has anything to work with.

It is open source, it makes zero network calls, and nothing you type ever leaves your browser. Every expected return, expense ratio, exchange rate and crisis figure is read off a published source with a date and a link, and the whole ledger is at the bottom of the page. Where I could not verify a number, it says so and leaves the cell blank instead of filling it in with something plausible.

It is a teaching tool and explicitly not investment advice. Tell me what I got wrong.
